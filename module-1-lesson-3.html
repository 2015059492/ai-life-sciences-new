AI Integration in Life Sciences Teaching Course

Module 1: Introduction to AI

Lesson 3

Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, teachers will be able to:

  1. Identify everyday tools that use AI.

  2. Explain how these tools help with daily tasks.

  3. Recognise that AI does not need advanced technical skills to use.

1. Everyday AI Tools You Already Use

Here are some common tools most teachers use regularly — all powered by AI:

  • Google Search
    Suggests the best results based on your past searches.

  • Predictive Text on WhatsApp/SMS
    Suggests words as you type.

  • Google Maps
    Chooses the fastest route using traffic data.

  • YouTube Recommendations
    Suggests videos based on your interests.

  • Gmail Smart Reply
    Suggests short responses like “Thank you” or “Noted.”

  • Google Photos
    Groups photos by faces, places, or events.

  • Voice Assistants (Siri, Google Assistant)
    Understand spoken questions and respond.

These tools show that AI is already part of daily life — not something new that teachers need to fear.

2. How These Tools Use AI

AI works in the background by learning from patterns. For example:

  • When you search “Life Sciences notes,” Google remembers what most people click and improves results.

  • When you type “I am on my…” WhatsApp predicts the next word based on common patterns.

  • YouTube learns the type of videos you usually watch to recommend similar ones.

You do not see the AI, but you experience its results.

3. Why This Matters for Teachers

When teachers recognise they already use AI daily, they realise:

  • AI is not new or scary — it’s familiar.

  • Using AI in teaching is not very different from using Google, WhatsApp, or YouTube.

  • AI tools for teaching follow the same patterns you already understand.

  • You can start small without needing technical skills.

This mindset shift helps build confidence for later lessons in this course.

4. Bridging Everyday AI to Classroom AI

Once you see AI in your everyday apps, it becomes easier to see how AI tools can support teaching, such as:

  • generating explanations

  • translating content into simpler language

  • creating worksheets

  • helping learners practise concepts

  • supporting multilingual classrooms

  • improving accessibility

AI becomes another tool — just like calculators, projectors, or digital textbooks.

Reflection Question

Which AI-powered tool do you use most often in your daily life, and how does it make your task easier?
