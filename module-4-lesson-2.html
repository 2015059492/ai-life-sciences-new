Module 4: Ethical and Responsible Use of AI in South African Schools

Lesson 2

Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, teachers will be able to:

  1. Develop clear and practical classroom rules for AI.

  2. Use AI responsibly while supporting CAPS Life Sciences outcomes.

  3. Encourage learners to critically engage with AI-generated content.

Creating Classroom AI Guidelines

Practical classroom rules help ensure AI is used safely and responsibly. Examples include:

  • “Do not share personal information when using AI tools.”

  • “Verify any scientific information provided by AI before using it in your work.”

  • “Use AI as a support tool, not a replacement for your own thinking.”

  • “Cite sources for any content created by AI.”

Tips for Implementation:

  • Introduce these rules at the start of the term and explain why they are important.

  • Model ethical AI use by showing learners how you check AI outputs and respect copyright.

  • Use real-life scenarios: Ask learners “What would you do if AI gives incorrect or biased information?”

  1. Integrating AI While Staying CAPS-Aligned
    AI should support curriculum goals, not replace hands-on learning. Here’s how to align AI use with CAPS outcomes:

  • Example: Photosynthesis (Grade 10)

    • AI-generated diagram: Use it to illustrate the process of photosynthesis.

    • Learner activity: Students still conduct a simple classroom experiment to observe plant response to light.

    • Reflection: Discuss with learners any differences between AI diagrams and observed results.

  • Example: Genetics (Grade 11)

    • AI-generated pedigree diagrams and explanations can save preparation time.

    • Learners still practice constructing their own diagrams to demonstrate understanding.

Key Principles:

  • Always cross-check AI outputs for accuracy.

  • Use AI to simplify complex concepts, provide alternative explanations, or generate additional practice questions.

  • Encourage learners to critique AI outputs critically, fostering digital literacy and scientific thinking.

  1. Practical Tips for Low-Resource Contexts

  • Print AI-generated diagrams or explanations for learners without devices.

  • Prepare AI-generated worksheets in advance for classroom use.

  • Use AI to simulate experiments digitally where lab equipment is limited.

Teacher Example:
For an ecology lesson, you could use AI to generate a simple food web diagram. Students then compare it to a real ecosystem observation activity, identifying similarities and differences. This shows learners that AI is a tool, but critical thinking is still essential.

Reflection Question
Choose one Life Sciences topic you teach. Describe how you would integrate AI responsibly in your lesson while ensuring it supports CAPS outcomes, protects learner data, and encourages critical thinking.
